        Phishing Awareness & Social Engineering Defense

        Phishing is the #1 initial access vector in modern enterprise security breaches. Attackers leverage cognitive shortcuts, fear, urgency, and misplaced authority to deceive targets into parting with credentials or executing malicious payloads.

        10 Red Flags to Spot Phishing Emails

        01

        Sender Address Spoofing

        Do not trust the friendly display name. Check the domain after the "@". Look for typosquatting (e.g., micros0ft.com instead of microsoft.com).

        02

        Artificial Urgency

        Phrases like "Act immediately", "Expires in 2 hours", or "Account lockout" are designed to create panic and bypass your rational critical thinking.

        03

        Raw IP or Masked Links

        Legitimate organizations rarely use raw IP addresses (e.g., http://198.51.100.10) or URL shorteners for official account verification.

        04

        HTTPS Does NOT Mean Safe

        HTTPS only encrypts data in transit. Threat actors frequently obtain free SSL/TLS certificates for malicious credential-harvesting websites.

        05

        Unsolicited Credential Requests

        Legitimate banks, universities, and IT departments will NEVER ask you to email or verify your password, PIN, or multi-factor code via an email link.

        06

        Dangerous / Double Extensions

        Be vigilant with attachments like .exe, .scr, .vbs, .bat, or disguised double extensions such as invoice.pdf.exe.

        07

        Generic Salutations

        Mass spearphishing often uses impersonal greetings like "Dear Customer", "Dear Member", or "Dear Valued Employee" instead of your actual name.

        08

        Unexpected Invoices / Wire Demands

        Fake invoices with urgent payment deadlines or CEO requests for discreet wire transfers or gift cards are classic social engineering hooks.

        09

        Unrealistic Rewards & Prizes

        If you allegedly won an international lottery, raffle, or cash bonus you never entered, it is always a credential or advance-fee scam.

        10

        Verify Out-of-Band

        When in doubt, never reply or click links in the message. Call the sender directly using a known verified phone number or visit the official site directly.
